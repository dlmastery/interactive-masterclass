# Cross-critique of the statistical proposal

7 October 2026. Read `statistics/source-analysis.md` and `statistics/route-proposal.md` after completing the initial geometry proposal. This is an author/researcher critique with substantial PCA prior knowledge, not a novice or independent human learning result. The two initial proposals were written separately, but overlap in primary sources and model capabilities; agreement is not learning evidence.

## Judgment

The statistical route is coherent and should improve the proposed main pilot. Its strongest contribution is making metric choice and the limits of the optimum part of the initial promise, then placing stability immediately after the global proof. Its 72 scene allocation reaches the main proof earlier than my 70–85+ scene allocation. Prefer that tighter ordering provisionally; do not shorten the actual algebra to meet a count.

There is no materially different main route here: both independently choose reconstruction → retained variance → covariance → stationarity → global bound. That convergence supports a sensible design hypothesis but does not satisfy a serious comparison of competing explanations by itself. Preserve the genuine alternative from the geometry proposal: measurement redundancy → change of basis → covariance diagonalization → ordered components → approximation. The alternative's best argument is that scientists want interpretable measurements, not necessarily compression. Its weakness is that decorrelation alone does not justify truncation or the top-k optimum. Compare it at the covariance/eigenvector bridge if the compression opening does not earn the chosen loss.

## Changes warranted before pilot

1. **Earn the mean, not just subtract it.** Scenes 9–12 show a translated cloud and an affine line through the mean, but the route needs a reason that the optimal line can pass there. For fixed orthogonal projector P and affine anchor a, decompose average residual loss into centered residual loss plus `||(I−P)(mu−a)||²`. The cross term vanishes because centered observations sum to zero. Choosing a=mu attains the minimum; anchors shifted along the same subspace describe the same affine set. This also repairs my initial proposal. The learner need not manipulate this entire expression before seeing the geometry, but the promised full derivation should show or explicitly invoke it.
2. **Do not let early encoding plant an origin assumption.** Scenes 5–8 precede centering. Use a ruler with a visibly marked anchor, or use an explicitly already-centered opening fixture. Later encode `wᵀ(x−mu)` and decode `mu+wz`. Ask the translated-point question before advancing; otherwise the learner may correctly dot-product against the wrong origin.
3. **State which subspace can remain stable.** Scenes 33–36 rightly distinguish unstable axes from a stable retained subspace. That requires a gap at the retained boundary. With eigenvalues 4.01, 4.00, 1, a two-dimensional leading subspace can be stable while its two axes rotate. With eigenvalues 4.01, 4.00 and k=1, the retained line itself can be unstable. Make k and the boundary gap visible. A round-cloud toggle without this distinction could teach a false reassurance.
4. **Keep the joint proof learner-visible.** The route's top-k spectral weights are the right global argument. Show why each weight is at most one and why their sum is k before claiming that allocating weight to the largest eigenvalues wins. Otherwise the proof hides the very constraint that excludes repeatedly taking the same top eigenvector. Require a held-out three-eigenvalue, k=2 allocation task.
5. **Preserve one fixed meaning of arrow length.** The source analysis correctly warns about variance-scaled arrows. Keep directions unit length and put variance in a separate bar/ellipse legend. Whitening is a new operation that rescales scores; show how it changes the metric or retain it as a clearly separate branch. Do not silently change arrow semantics at the SVD or biplot scene.

## Critical pilot refinement

Use the statistical pilot's compact opening and global-bound sequence, but include one explicit covariance construction immediately before its scenes 25–32. Supply a two-feature score, expand its square, and ask which data summary coefficient changes for opposite-tilt clouds. A ready-made covariance matrix would bypass the hardest learning obligation.

Add two neutral discriminators: a translated held-out point for affine reconstruction, and the eigenvalues 4.01, 4.00, 1 with k toggled between one and two for stability. These identify a conceptual failure rather than reward recognizing the supplied formula. Keep the planned low-variance predictive signal transfer. Require the learner to state the objective before choosing a method; a mathematically optimal compression can fail the operational goal.

If this pilot fails, first revise the representation or ordering. Do not add more foundation slides automatically. If learners reject the compression task as arbitrary despite understanding the error accounting, try the measurement-redundancy alternative with the same final proof obligation and compare visible reasoning warrants. No route is declared superior before that comparison.
