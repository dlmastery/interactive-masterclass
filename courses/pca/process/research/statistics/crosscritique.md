# Crosscritique after independent proposal

7 October 2026. Read `../geometry/route-proposal.md` only after saving `route-proposal.md`. Both proposers have advanced PCA knowledge; neither is evidence of novice learning.

The two initial proposals converge on reconstruction first, covariance derived from projected squares, constrained stationarity, and a separate global spectral bound. That agreement does not create two materially different routes or validate superiority. The serious alternative remains measurement redundancy → coordinate change → decorrelation → ordered axes, with reconstruction and its guarantee subsequently supplied. The coordinator should preserve that alternative rather than count two versions of the same route as strategic breadth.

The geometry route's strongest contribution is the operation `Cw` interpreted as a sum of data vectors weighted by their projected scores. This could connect the cloud to the constrained gradient more effectively than displaying a matrix derivative. Its risk is introducing a second weighted-vector construction before learners distinguish scalar score, direction, and reconstructed point. Pilot it with one point contribution, then two, then the whole sum; do not introduce all three quantities with the same arrow style. The learner should be able to say what changes and what is held fixed.

The biggest concern is allocation. Geometry places the global bound around scenes 51–57 after roughly forty scenes of preparation. My allocation reaches it around 29–32. Neither count establishes correct pacing, but the user explicitly prioritizes the covariance-to-eigenvector derivation. The pilot should report elapsed learner time to that payoff and whether earlier scenes support it. Do not cut the geometry blindly; consolidate repeated practice or create in-place prerequisite branches if the core proof arrives too late.

Both openings may over-earn reconstruction while under-earning why this metric matters. A tracker or sensor cloud is concrete, but synthetic coordinates can feel like a puzzle selected to justify the algorithm. The very first payoff should show actual reconstructed readings and their error, and explicitly invite the later question of whether that error is the right loss. Do not manufacture a deployment story. A short label-relevance preview can promise a consequential decision without prematurely teaching alternatives.

The geometry proposal's scientific route competitor deserves an actual pilot if the opening fails. Decorrelation is a natural goal for redundant measurements, but it leaves an obligation: uncorrelated axes alone do not say which dimensions to discard or why squared reconstruction is optimal. Every serious final route must meet the same proof and task-choice contract.

Suggested synthesis: retain the stable geometric scene objects, use the metric-choice opening, test the `Cw` operation at the hardest bridge, reserve the full global proof in the core, and bring task relevance back before the course becomes a catalogue of alternatives. Use source-supported Seurat rare-component sensitivity and the PCR/PLS counterexample as discriminating transfer work. These are design judgments to test, not completed learning outcomes.
