# Explanatory craft: choose techniques for the learner's inference

Read while comparing teaching routes and designing a critical pilot. This reference turns selected creator practices into operations that can be reviewed. It complements the complete content contract, tutoring research, explanation-depth map, and narration reference. It does not replace a topic's broad research or establish a ranking of creators.

The linked sources are creator guidance, original companion materials, or identified research. The protocol below is our adaptation, not a claim that every creator follows it. Choose techniques for this topic and audience; do not impose all of them on every scene. Study the reasoning and construction without copying a creator's script, distinctive artwork, or delivery identity.

## Moves worth considering

| Teaching move | Operation and useful check | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| A question with a visible payoff | Open with a puzzle, decision, or attainable subproblem. Revisit it when the learner has the needed model. Ask what they can now explain that they could not explain at the start. [S1, S3] | An intriguing hook that the lesson never resolves; withholding needed context for suspense. |
| Examples that earn abstraction | Let a concrete case reveal the relationship; introduce its precise name and framework when useful. Map each important term back to the case. [S1, S2] | Delaying essential definitions mechanically, or leaving the learner with a picture but no usable formal account. |
| A sequence driven by unresolved questions | Explain why the current account is insufficient and what the next idea resolves. Keep the route coherent through returns to the original problem. [S3, W1, W2] | A list of topics connected only by “next”; fabricated history or a false claim that there was only one possible solution. |
| Prediction before a reveal | Where readiness permits, ask for an outcome and a reason. Compare it with the result and revise the explanation. Supply a model or hint first when the learner lacks an entry point. [S4] | Endless guessing, compulsory interactivity, or a dramatic reveal whose mechanism remains hidden. |
| A plausible wrong model given a fair test | Show why someone could believe it, then use evidence that distinguishes it from a better model. State the replacement explicitly. [S5] | Humiliation, fake student mistakes, or implying that every topic requires manufactured confusion. |
| Visual change carrying an inference | State what the viewer should notice; align drawing, motion, pointer, and narration with it. Preserve useful before/after states. [S1] | Moving equations decoratively or implying a visual transformation is a mathematical proof. |
| Construction when construction matters | Reveal a diagram, argument, or procedure in steps when the intermediate states explain a choice. Otherwise provide a stable clear representation. [S6] | Treating handwriting, animation, or polished graphics as inherently better teaching. |
| Respectful, prepared conversation | Rehearse for one learner with the stated prerequisites. Preserve precise claims while making the voice natural. Keep reviewed notes even when delivery permits variation. [S6] | Expert shorthand, condescension, unprepared improvisation, or mechanical script reading. |
| A check that can expose a mistake | After deriving or implementing something, use a distinct check and explain what disagreement would mean. Keep numerical illustration, implementation checks, empirical tests, and proof separate. [W2] | Trusting a plausible output, testing with the same faulty logic twice, or claiming a check establishes more than it does. |
| Help that preserves the pending question | Expand the prerequisite in place, apply it immediately, and restore the original task and state. [S7] | A glossary detour that loses the learner's reason for needing the term. |
| Guided work that opens into agency | Move from an entry task to learner-chosen cases, questions, or constructions. Give a useful starting action and ways to inspect results. [S4] | A large unexplained sandbox, or controls whose only purpose is to make the learner click. |
| Practice with different reasoning jobs | Include explanation, procedure, diagnosis, and a meaningful challenge where appropriate. Let the learner attempt before consulting useful feedback. [S8, W3] | Repeating numerical variants as the only practice, or mistaking recognition of a polished explanation for independent ability. |
| Simplifications reviewed after rendering | Record consequential omissions and possible false inferences. Review the plain argument and then the actual illustration/timed scene against its sources. [S9] | Assuming a sourced script makes every metaphor, scale, or animation accurate. |
| A closing that transfers ownership | Resolve the opening problem, ask the learner to reconstruct the central idea, then invite a new question they can investigate with it. [S3; explanation-depth.md] | Ending with applause, a slogan, or further videos as the only next step. |

These strengthen existing requirements rather than introduce a form per sentence. A curious detour or human story may serve motivation; explain its relevance and test whether it interrupts the reasoning. Do not impose a creator's exact runtime, number of drafts, catchphrase, or production style. The user's quality-first preference remains controlling.

## Welch Labs: problem continuity, representation, and verification

Welch is an explicit reference in this user's creator landscape. Inspect the series relevant to the next topic; do not treat a familiar channel name as completed research.

- **Need before formal machinery.** The Imaginary Numbers series connects the need for a wider mathematical account with its historical development and applications. Adapt the relationship between problem and new tool; verify historical specifics independently. [W1]
- **One case that evolves.** Neural Networks Demystified carries a small prediction task through successive machinery and later failure analysis. Keep concept IDs, data meaning, notation, and visual referents consistent as the learner gains a stronger account. Where code matters, map the visible object to its mathematical symbol, code identifier, and shape. [W2]
- **A visible obstacle motivates the next method.** Show the weakness of a current approach before adding complexity. Reconstructed discovery is a teaching route, not a claim about the historical order of discovery. [W2]
- **Representations with continuity.** The workbook uses sequential figure states to connect a familiar graph to an expanded view. When adapting this, label axes, projection, and omitted quantities at the reveal; retain the starting state for comparison. [W3]
- **A conjecture linked to a second representation.** The complex-plane assignment asks learners to plot operands and products before the next lesson relates algebraic multiplication to geometry. Adapt this as a supported comparison, a learner conjecture, the relevant justification, and a fresh test. Examples alone do not establish a general theorem. [W4]
- **Derive, inspect, and challenge.** The neural-network sequence places numerical gradient checking before training, then addresses generalization. Adapt verification and failure analysis to the domain rather than copying old code or numerical rules. [W2]
- **The viewer becomes an explainer.** Workbook activities include discussion, procedural work, critical reasoning, and challenge. Our adaptation adds fresh audience-specific teach-back and transfer using explanation-depth.md. [W3]

Separate a useful teaching pattern from the correctness of every source statement. Legacy code, data-split practices, heuristics, historical claims, and simplified diagrams need their own checks. Preserve central qualifications in the scene where the inference happens; a much later correction may arrive too late. Never promote an admired creator to an infallible authority.

## Fit these into the existing loops

**Strategy loop:** for each serious route, identify its opening question, pivotal change in understanding, running case, practical decision, and closing payoff. Select creator techniques that support those demands. Record a short reason for any significant incompatibility; a coherent route is preferable to collecting every admired feature.

**Scene repair loop:** name the learner's likely model, intended inference, evidence/representation, and expected reasoning. When review finds a gap, change the mechanism that should teach it. Cosmetic revisions do not count as repairing a missing inference. Use the existing bridge contract and scene IDs.

**Learner loop:** capture a prediction, construction, explanation, or decision; respond to its reasoning; reduce assistance and try a changed case. The learner may propose a valid route the author did not anticipate. Ask for clarification when the answer is ambiguous rather than forcing a canned misconception.

**Review:** check the opening promise was fulfilled, adjacent steps have a reason, visual and spoken claims agree, major simplifications are honest, and the ending gives the learner explanatory work. Use neutral tasks and actual artifacts. Viewing comfort, completion, and learning are distinct. Record which source was read, watched, listened to, or executed; text inspection does not establish pacing or performance.
