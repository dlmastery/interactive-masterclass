# Creator practices to test in the reusable teaching process

6 October 2026. Author-informed research note. This is a focused process comparison, not a popularity ranking, a course build, or a learning-effectiveness study. No course or installed skill was edited. Creator descriptions establish their stated practice; the proposed adaptations below still need topic-specific testing.

## 1. Sal Khan: prepared reasoning in a respectful conversational voice

**Primary source:** [Khan Academy, “Blackboard style videos” transcript](https://www.khanacademy.org/transcript/xbb00de6e). Inspected indexed official transcript text from 0:00–9:36; direct opening returned an empty text shell. No playback or listening. Publication date was not exposed.

**Observed advice:** At 0:40–4:10 Khan recommends speaking to a person naturally, avoiding both skipped “obvious” steps and a patronizing voice. At 4:20–5:37 he discusses diagrams, color, and visible drawing. At 5:40–8:05 he stresses preparing the explanation mentally; writing can help preparation, and a capable script reader can actively engage with its meaning. At 8:20–9:26 he favors navigable chunks.

**Adaptation:** Rehearse each difficult cue as an explanation to one prepared but unfamiliar learner. Check for missing reasoning and condescension separately. Use progressive construction when the act of building a representation matters; retain readable checkpoints.

**Use and failure:** Useful for derivations and worked procedures. Do not convert his preferences into proof that handwriting outperforms graphics, a ban on scripts, or a ten-minute ceiling. Spontaneity can introduce errors; natural delivery still needs reviewed content. His claims about what resonates are creator judgments here, not independently tested efficacy.

## 2. Kurzgesagt: test the claims after the story becomes persuasive

**Primary source:** [Lizzy Steib, Head of Research, “How research and factchecking work at Kurzgesagt”](https://medium.com/@Kurzgesagt/how-research-and-factchecking-work-at-kurzgesagt-f5b239188255), 28 March 2023. Inspected the accessible article, particularly Factchecking, Sources, Working with experts, and Quality assurance. No video viewed.

**Reported practice:** Reviewers inspect the plain script statement by statement, compare sources, recompute estimates, and test whether compression misleads. The source sheet follows video order. External specialists challenge the team’s attachment to its story; contested subjects receive differing expert perspectives. Content review continues through sketches, storyboards, and the first timed render, including labels, motion, and consistency. Steib distinguishes playful anthropomorphism from physically impossible motion that viewers could mistake for fact.

**Adaptation:** Pair each consequential simplification with the omitted condition, likely false inference, and a visible boundary or counterexample. Let a reviewer challenge the argument without the persuasive final artwork. Recheck scientific meaning after rendering.

**Use and failure:** Valuable for animation, analogy, quantities, and controversial topics. A cited script can still become a misleading diagram. Expert agreement does not automatically settle disagreement; document scope and dissent. This is a dated organizational account, not evidence that its entire production method causes learning gains.

## 3. Smarter Every Day: build a route from a concrete knowledge gap

**Primary source:** [Destin Sandlin’s official video/series guide](https://www.smartereveryday.com/videos). Inspected the complete page text; no embedded episode watched or listened to. Publication date was not exposed.

**Reported examples:** Sandlin describes beginning helicopter exploration with an incorrect belief about lift control, investigating bird flight through observation and practitioner contact, and learning butterfly mechanics with a breeder and specialists. He explicitly separates watching his helicopter series from pilot training.

**Adaptation:** Anchor a lesson in a plausible initial model and an observation it cannot explain. Show what evidence changes the model; then test a different case. Preserve the boundary between explaining a skill and performing it.

**Use and failure:** Useful for empirical mechanisms, troubleshooting, and craft. A compelling demonstration may not discriminate rival causes. State what was controlled, what remains uncertain, and what the example cannot establish. Do not stage fake ignorance, imply the series was inspected, or copy its promotional mastery language. These adaptations are inferences from the creator’s examples, not a claimed formal teaching recipe.

## Small additions worth integrating

These are our proposed review operations, rather than further claims about the creators:

1. **Delivery-mode choice:** preserve exact reviewed notes and cue obligations. Choose a performed script, guided live explanation, or hybrid according to precision and medium. Evaluate the actual delivery against the intended reasoning. Conversational does not mean unprepared; publication quality does not require reciting mechanically.
2. **Construction witness:** for a difficult visual, ask which learner inference depends on seeing its construction. If none does, use a clear stable diagram. If one does, expose that construction and retain a comparison state; motion alone is not an explanation.
3. **Simplification record:** attach the important qualification, likely misconception, and diagnostic case to the scene. Review the rendered visual as a scientific claim, not merely as decoration checked against an accurate script.
4. **Evidence-changing moment:** record the initial model, decisive observation, revised model, and what remains unresolved. Ask a learner to say why the observation changes the explanation, rather than merely repeat the surprising outcome.

Keep these inside the existing scene contract and review evidence. Do not add a new platform, impose creator-specific draft or duration counts, require every scene to begin with a misconception, or claim that adopting these practices guarantees learning or reach. The common test is whether the chosen move makes a needed inference available and survives actual review.
