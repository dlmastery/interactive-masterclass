# Tutoring research for the reusable protocol

6 October 2026. **Author-informed research synthesis:** I reviewed the earlier course and participated in its retrospective. I am an AI reviewer, not a demonstrated expert human tutor. This note changes no installed skill or course. It distinguishes published findings, theoretical frameworks, and our proposed design inferences. It is a targeted primary/official-source review, not a systematic review or proof of generalization to 1,000 topics.

## Seven inspected sources

**1. IES/What Works Clearinghouse (2007), [Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf).** Official evidence-based practice guide. Inspected recommendation summary, evidence ratings, worked-example discussion, and Recommendation 7 with its technical evidence discussion. The guide supports spaced exposure, alternating worked examples and problems, integrating concrete and abstract representations, and explanatory questions. It rates recommendations differently; deep explanatory questioning receives strong evidence, several others moderate. Crucially, its deep-question recommendation assumes learners possess basic topic knowledge. **Application:** introduce a useful model and prerequisites before demanding explanations; later ask why, why-not, comparison, and evidence questions. **Limit:** this synthesis does not validate a particular AI prompt, universal interaction pattern, or mandatory delay schedule. The stated practices still need subject-specific implementation.

**2. Collins, Brown, and Newman (1987), [Cognitive Apprenticeship](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED284181.pdf).** Foundational design framework with reading, writing, and mathematics exemplars, not a randomized test of our proposed protocol. Inspected “Schooling and Apprenticeship,” reciprocal-teaching discussion, “Method,” scaffolding/fading, and framework table. It locates skills in meaningful activity and makes expert strategies observable through modeling, coaching, articulation, reflection, and eventual independent exploration. Scaffolding depends on diagnosing the current difficulty; students progressively assume responsibility. **Application:** show how a capable practitioner recognizes a situation, chooses a method, checks it, and changes course—not only the finished answer. **Limit:** its authors acknowledge that expert strategies cannot all be exhaustively formalized. A skill file cannot guarantee the situational judgment of a human practitioner.

**3. Black and Wiliam (2009), [Developing the Theory of Formative Assessment](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/9119063/Black2009_Developing_the_theory_of_formative_assessment.pdf).** Inspected author manuscript’s “A unifying basis,” five strategies, operational definition, and discussion of discipline-specific norms. The framework links intended learning, evidence of current understanding, and instructional decisions. Assessment is formative because evidence changes the next action, not because a quiz appears. Learners and peers have roles alongside teachers. **Application:** record the attempt and uncertainty, interpret the reason, then choose a targeted next step. **Limit:** this is theoretical synthesis, not a causal estimate for every feedback technique; good explanations and success criteria differ between mathematics and history.

**4. Chi and Wylie (2014), [The ICAP Framework](https://education.asu.edu/sites/default/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf).** Inspected engagement definitions/examples, knowledge-change assumptions, empirical-summary discussion, and Section 3 caveats. It distinguishes manipulating presented material from generating inferences and jointly developing ideas. It explicitly treats the proposed knowledge-change mechanisms as hypotheses and discusses exceptions involving assessment, domain, and task differences. Learners lacking an appropriate schema may not bootstrap understanding through explanation alone. **Application:** inspect what the student must infer or produce; clicking or talking is insufficient evidence. **Limit:** “interactive” in ICAP does not mean a UI has sliders, and a higher category does not guarantee greater learning for every task. Avoid compulsory dialogue or unsupported discovery.

**5. Kestin et al. (2025), [AI Tutoring Outperforms In-Class Active Learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6).** Primary randomized crossover study in introductory Harvard physics. Inspected “Learning gains: post-test scores,” “Study design,” “Designing successful student-AI interactions,” and “Context, limitations, and future directions,” using the publicly available [article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/) through Europe PMC’s full-text XML. Its structured AI condition produced higher immediate post-test performance across two lessons than its active-classroom comparison. The system used instructor-crafted solutions and platform-controlled sequencing; prompting alone did not reliably keep multipart scaffolding in order. **Application:** verify content and implement the actual teaching sequence rather than assuming a persona prompt supplies it. **Limit:** this tests a package including expert materials, videos, individual pacing, and setting. It does not isolate a winning prompt, establish delayed retention, or prove replacement of human teachers across disciplines.

**6. Bastani et al. (2025), [Generative AI Without Guardrails Can Harm Learning](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122).** Primary classroom-randomized study across four sessions in one Turkish high school. Inspected “Experimental Design,” “Main Results,” “Student Perception,” and “Discussion,” using the [published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/) through Europe PMC XML. Both GPT conditions improved assisted practice. On the subsequent unassisted exam, GPT Base’s normalized-score effect was −0.054, about 17% relative to the control mean; GPT Tutor’s −0.004 estimate was statistically indistinguishable from control. Tutor safeguards supplied teacher solutions/common errors and encouraged hints. **Application:** distinguish assisted accomplishment from independent capability; test after removing assistance. **Limit:** guardrails mitigated observed harm but did not demonstrate improved unassisted learning here. This is short-term mathematics evidence from a particular deployment/model era, not proof that all AI harms learning or that withholding every answer is optimal.

**7. Wang et al. (2025 version), [Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/ai24_1054_v2.pdf).** Primary field experiment/working paper. Inspected abstract, exit-ticket result, limitations, and sampling/outcome notes. This version reports more than 700 tutors and 1,000 students; do not mix it with earlier 900/1,800 descriptions. Tutor access increased session exit-ticket passing by four percentage points, with larger gains for lower-rated tutors. Humans selected and edited suggestions. **Application:** support a human tutor’s next-action judgment and preserve override/ownership. **Limit:** the trial estimates offering the bundled tool, not the causal value of each strategy or a standalone AI tutor. The paper reports no statistically significant end-of-year test improvement and notes exposure, demographic, and modality constraints.

The two article XML files are saved beside this note as inspection receipts. I inspected text, not videos or animations. The contrasting AI results support testing design and use conditions; they do not establish a universally superior human/AI format.

## Proposed common protocol — a design inference

These recommendations synthesize the sources and our retrospective. Their complete combination has **not** been experimentally validated.

### Outer loop: design and revise the teaching approach

1. **Specify useful capability.** Establish the learner’s purpose, preparation, context, access needs, and desired depth. State what they should explain, predict, decide, create, or perform afterward. Determine whether the situation calls for learning, task completion, or both; a worked answer can be appropriate without claiming it establishes competence.
2. **Map the topic’s meaning and use.** Cover why it matters, what it is, how it works, when/where it applies, applications, limitations, failure modes, mitigations, alternatives, and best practices. Connect these through questions and decisions. Mark coverage as taught, practiced, assessed, optional, or legitimately inapplicable; do not create a checklist paragraph for every heading. Scope depth to the requested capability while preserving all materially relevant obligations.
3. **Choose a coherent route.** Inspect authoritative subject sources and strong explanations. Compare their purposes, prerequisites, central representations, inferential steps, omitted cases, and learning claims. Select a route that explains the mechanism; retain compatible techniques with an explicit job. Produce an opening, hardest conceptual bridge with its local context, and meaningful transfer test before expanding the course.
4. **Review the educational hypothesis.** Ask what wrong model the learner could retain despite following every screen. Test correctness, continuity, attention demands, applications, boundary decisions, and task performance separately. Human tutors or target learners can reveal preparation and interpretation errors that AI reviewers may miss. If unavailable, retain the uncertainty rather than renaming simulated review as learning evidence.
5. **Repair the cause and retest affected claims.** A new analogy, representation, worked example, ordering, or feedback branch should address a named failure and predict an observable improvement. Reopen dependent material; preserve unaffected valid evidence. Expand only when critical explanatory obligations have adequate evidence for the claimed delivery level.

### Inner loop: respond to one learner at one difficulty

**Orient → elicit or demonstrate → interpret → respond → fade → transfer → revisit.** This is a decision loop, not a fixed seven-step ritual.

- Start from the current task and learner’s attempted explanation, prediction, performance, or uncertainty. If no workable model exists, give a concise worked example or missing prerequisite before demanding a guess. Preserve the original question and return point.
- Interpret more than correctness: distinguish a slip, missing prerequisite, inappropriate strategy, misconceived relationship, ambiguous wording, and a defensible alternative. Ask a discriminating question when uncertain; do not invent a diagnosis from one answer.
- Choose a proportionate response: point to a relevant feature, contrast cases, supply a hint, model a step, explain directly, or change representation. Make its connection to the learner’s reasoning explicit. Supportive feedback should be specific and usable, not generic praise or an endless sequence of questions.
- Return responsibility to the learner. Request an unaided reconstruction, justification, or application; then vary the situation so copying the worked path is insufficient. Test both using the method and recognizing when to avoid or modify it.
- Revisit important ideas after a meaningful delay or later topic, where feasible and authorized. Record assistance and remaining uncertainty. A correct answer with an invalid reason remains unresolved; a plausible explanation is not proof of durable capability.

For a **human tutor**, provide the route, diagnostic possibilities, worked cases, and assessment evidence while preserving judgment about pacing, affect, and exceptions. For **AI**, also require verified reference content/solutions, reliable state and return points, uncertainty handling, and tested response branches; a static course must disclose its inability to diagnose arbitrary responses. For **human+AI**, present suggestions with their intended purpose and let the human select, edit, or reject them. These implementation duties are our inference from the evidence, not proof of equivalent tutoring competence.

## Domain-general adaptation

| Topic family | Central reasoning to teach | Useful application/limit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics/formal methods | Derive or justify a relationship; maintain assumptions and invariants | A novel problem plus a tempting method whose conditions fail |
| Empirical science | Link observations, mechanism/model, intervention, and uncertainty | Predict a changed system; distinguish rival explanations and model limits |
| History/source evaluation | Distinguish source, inference, corroboration, context, and uncertainty | Evaluate a conflicting source without forcing a single predetermined interpretation |
| Writing/design | Connect audience, purpose, choices, constraints, and revision | Revise a new artifact, justify tradeoffs, compare legitimate alternatives |
| Practical procedures | Recognize conditions, execute, monitor, recover, and escalate | Perform with reduced support; diagnose a realistic failure and choose mitigation |

The stable unit is a consequential learner inference. Its witness may be a proof, experiment, source comparison, critique, or performance. Do not force every topic into geometric intuition, formulas, one correct verbal answer, or gamified controls.

## Quality first without collage or endless review

“Best of 30” is a search strategy, not an educational design. Combining appealing fragments can produce contradictory assumptions, notation, depth, and metaphors. Maintain a coherent explanatory backbone; add a technique only when it resolves a specific learner difficulty and survives integration review. Search breadth should follow unresolved questions and diversity of approaches, not a mandatory source count or the prestige of creators.

Perfection cannot be the acceptance test: it is neither observable nor falsifiable. Use the requested capability and critical obligations as the quality target. Do not impose arbitrary time, slide, or attempt cutoffs that silently sacrifice difficult teaching work. Continue while a material failure has a plausible causal repair and a meaningful next test. After repeated unchanged failures, explicitly change the hypothesis, obtain different evidence/expertise, or report the unresolved dependency. Repeating the same review of the same artifact with the same criteria is not progress.

Completion means the agreed content and instructional obligations are met at the honestly stated evidence level. Unavailable learner or delayed evidence remains unverified; it does not authorize an unsupported learning claim. Reusability across 1,000 topics is an architecture and evaluation ambition requiring diverse domain tests, not a claim established by these seven sources or two successful mini-lessons.
