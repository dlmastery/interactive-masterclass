## Disposition rules

For each case report PASS, REVISE, or UNVERIFIED for the tested claim with cited candidate wording. PASS requires coherent purpose, a supported hard bridge, connected requested breadth, a genuine event-cued teaching passage, substantive accuracy, and honest evidence labels. A material false claim, absent conceptual bridge, false source/media/learner evidence, or failure to adapt the contract to the domain blocks PASS. Missing actual listening, rendering, learning, or delayed evidence is UNVERIFIED, not automatic failure of a script-only test. Do not combine scores to hide one case’s critical failure.

The 500-word total and 100–150 spoken-word bounds are explicit test constraints. Count prose with a reasonable stated method; exclude references and bracketed cues from the spoken tally. A minor counting ambiguity is not a conceptual failure. A very short slogan or long lecture replacing the requested passage requires revision. A static plan or future-tense promise alone cannot count as the actual passage. Topic cases are small tests; a demand for a full 30-video workflow here would violate scope. No full-course completeness claim follows from passing excerpts.

## Shared content and narration obligations

- Purpose describes a capability or consequential intellectual/practical decision, not “learn about X.” Why, when, and where connect to the method/interpretation, not unrelated opening enthusiasm.
- The plan protects the central bridge and places any missing background at its use. An attractive opening cannot compensate for an unsupported middle.
- Application names an actor, task, method/evidence role, and success or interpretation criterion. Current implementation/adoption claims need direct supporting evidence. A stable foundational fact need not be made artificially recent.
- A significant limit has a causal/evidential mechanism, observable diagnostic with limits, response, residual cost, and comparison to a serious alternative. One fully connected chain can satisfy this excerpt; no exhaustive encyclopedia is required.
- The fresh check requires choosing, diagnosing, explaining, or rejecting in a changed case. Merely “Any questions?” or recalling a vocabulary label does not establish transfer design.
- Spoken notes point to specific visible/audible referents as they change, include useful sequencing or pauses, and explicitly articulate the key inference. “Show an animation here” with generic narration is inadequate. Cues need not have arbitrary timestamps. The script should work aloud without requiring symbol decoding or reading an unseen paragraph.
- Sources, audience assumptions, planned media, and untested claims are honest. Do not reward an impressive bibliography without claim support. No promise of human mastery or YouTube superiority is warranted.

## Case 1: gradient descent

**Critical bridge:** the negative gradient gives a locally descending direction under appropriate differentiability/nonzero-gradient conditions; it does not guarantee that every finite step lowers the objective or reaches the global minimum. The worked event must make direction and step length separately visible. A valid one-dimensional example or clearly stated local approximation is enough; no required notation or numerical dataset.

An evaluator cross-check is f(x)=x² at x=2: gradient 4; x_new=2−4η. With η=0.25, x_new=1 and loss decreases; with η=1.5, x_new=−4 and loss increases. The candidate need not use this example. Confirm its own numbers and visual story. Do not require the narrator to introduce multidimensional gradient calculus to this audience.

**Breadth:** connects model parameters, objective/error, derivative, update, and stopping/validation to a real fitting task. A modern use could be neural-network training, but current use must be grounded in primary papers or official documentation. Saying “AI uses this everywhere” is not adequate. Distinguish illustrative scalar loss from a documented deployment.

**Limit-response-choice:** a too-large step causing oscillation/divergence with observed loss behavior and reduced/adapted step or line search is a valid chain. If discussing noisy stochastic updates, do not infer that every single minibatch loss increase is failure. If discussing nonconvexity, do not promise that small steps avoid every saddle/local minimum. Validation error is a distinct model-quality issue from training-objective optimization.

**Alternatives:** compare at least two serious options across the same relevant criteria or clearly different task regimes. Examples: closed-form/linear algebra solution for suitable small problems, curvature-based Newton/quasi-Newton for suitable smooth objectives, minibatch SGD/momentum or Adam for large/noisy training. A named list is insufficient. Do not crown Adam, Newton, or gradient descent universally best. At least one choice should have an explicit cost or condition under which it loses.

**Narration test:** in the spoken passage, learners see a tangent/local slope and a finite candidate move, then a comparison that earns the conclusion about step size. A hiker metaphor without an actual objective/update relation or a formula read aloud without the depicted operation does not pass.

Relevant screening sources: original Adam paper https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980 and official optimizer documentation https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/optim.html. They are not a sufficient source for every candidate claim. Verify any further niche or current claim against its actual source.

## Case 2: did Rome fall in 476?

**Critical bridge:** distinguish an event/date marking a political change in the Western imperial system from the claim that all Roman society, institutions, or the whole Roman Empire disappeared simultaneously. The Eastern continuation is a useful counterexample, but need not be the only bridge. Rome the city, western imperial rule, and the Roman world are not interchangeable entities.

**Interpretation and evidence:** plan at least one concrete evidence category/source, provenance/context, and an inference it can or cannot support. Serious competing collapse/transformation interpretations must differ in what they explain, geographical/social scale, or evidence, rather than just offering two unsupported opinions. Do not demand a balanced verdict between a defensible view and a fabricated fringe claim. A genuine scholarly dispute may remain unresolved.

**Breadth adaptation:** “application” can mean evaluating a museum label, historical analogy, or textbook explanation. “Diagnosis” can mean detecting a claim that overgeneralizes from an emperor’s deposition, one region, elite testimony, or selective material evidence. “Mitigation” may mean comparing independently relevant sources, narrowing a claim, distinguishing scales, or exposing uncertainty. It cannot mean repairing ancient history or optimizing an empire with a formula. Explain a cost or residual limitation such as missing evidence, uneven geographical survival, or disagreement about which changes matter.

**Alternatives:** compare serious interpretations or periodizations on explanatory consequences/evidence, not a technique shopping list. A present-day analogy should explicitly limit what follows from similarity. Do not imply a universal law that any modern state with one Roman feature must collapse.

**Narration test:** a dated marker, map, artifact/source comparison, or timeline must change the warranted inference while the narrator identifies the referents. An atmospheric montage with “history is complicated” is insufficient. Do not require a primary-source quotation; invented quotes or images treated as actual inspected artifacts block PASS. Chronological/political details must be checked and qualified at the level the candidate uses them.

Screening source for broad chronology: Metropolitan Museum of Art, https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-roman-empire-27-b-c-393-a-d . Museum synthesis is authoritative orientation, not itself an ancient primary source or proof of a specific cause. Do not require one chosen scholarly interpretation as the hidden correct answer.

## Case 3: clear bedroom narration

**Critical bridge:** a gain/volume change scales an already captured mixture; it does not by itself improve the direct-speech-to-room-reflection balance. Moving/aiming the microphone, changing the room/placement, and suitable acoustic absorption can alter that balance before capture. Avoid the absolute claim that postprocessing can never reduce reverb; distinguish mitigation from guaranteed restoration.

**Practical demonstration:** specify the same short spoken phrase recorded under two controlled conditions, what changes and what stays stable, and what the learner listens for. Match perceived playback loudness or otherwise guard against mistaking louder for clearer. Use pauses/word tails or another intelligibility cue that reveals reflection. No real recordings are required in this request, but they must be labeled planned.

**Diagnosis and response:** distinguish room reverberation from background noise or clipping when recommending remedies. Reduced input gain can prevent overload but cannot remove already clipped distortion by merely lowering playback volume. Close placement can improve the direct/room balance, yet can add plosives, proximity coloration for suitable microphones, or level inconsistency. Avoid a universal exact distance/angle for phones and all USB microphones. Re-record, listen, and adjust to the actual device.

**Alternatives/tradeoffs:** compare a feasible room/placement change, improvised soft furnishings/treatment, and/or careful processing or a different recording location. No purchase or magical “better microphone fixes the room” shortcut. Identify a realistic cost such as convenience, speaking consistency, appearance, coloration, or artifacts. Good practical advice should enable a decision with existing resources.

**Narration test:** cues name A/B audio examples and the controlled recording conditions, provide listening time, and explain a perceptual difference that warrants the next action. The narrator need not perform an impossible physical demonstration in text. Pure setup instructions without an audible/visible conceptual comparison do not pass.

Screening sources: Shure’s technical publication https://www.shure.com/en-US/docs/education/Microphone-Techniques-for-Recording and critical-distance guidance https://service.shure.com/articles/en_US/Knowledge/critical-distance-and-microphone-placement . These support placement/acoustic reasoning; manufacturer authority does not establish every product benefit. Do not require brand purchases.
