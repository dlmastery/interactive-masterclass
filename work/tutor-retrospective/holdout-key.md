# Held-out acceptance key — open only after candidate outputs exist

Prepared before seeing candidate outputs. This is an artifact review instrument, not a validated measure of human learning. Parent/coordinator should freeze candidate outputs and their provenance before reading this file. If a candidate agent sees this key, label that test contaminated and do not call its score held-out. Sharing the normal lesson requests and broad skill criteria is expected; sharing these specific scoring expectations is not.

Score each dimension 0 (absent/incorrect), 1 (partial/asserted), or 2 (clear and supported). A candidate must have no material factual error, earn 2 on the hard bridge and practical purpose, and score at least 10/12. Report scores separately; do not average one topic’s failure away. If a requirement is arguable, preserve the objection and quote the candidate’s text. These thresholds are predeclared local screening rules, not validated cutoffs for excellent teaching.

## Prompt 1 — six dimensions

1. **Purpose and decision.** The learner should leave able to distinguish a historical summary from an estimate relevant to their own quiet/busy schedule. “Learn weighted averages” alone is not a purpose. The conclusion should recommend A for the stipulated half-and-half schedule under the stated use of last week’s period-specific averages; it must not claim guaranteed faster service on every future visit.
2. **Hard bridge: composition creates the reversal.** Explain concretely that A’s records mostly sample its busy period and B’s mostly its quiet period, so the two overall averages weight different circumstances differently. The learner should be able to reconstruct this from visit counts or total waiting minutes. Merely announcing Simpson’s paradox, saying statistics mislead, or writing a weighted-mean formula without the composition mechanism earns at most 1.
3. **Numerical correctness and continuity.** Historical totals are A: 20 + 900 = 920 waiting minutes over 100 visits, mean 9.2; B: 270 + 120 = 390 over 100, mean 3.9. For the learner’s 50/50 schedule, predicted mean waits are A: 6; B: 7.5. Both the historical comparison and the matched-mixture comparison answer legitimate but different questions. It is acceptable to show fewer arithmetic lines if all required links remain reconstructable.
4. **Representation and prerequisites.** A compact count table, 100-visit picture, or equivalent should keep “number of visits,” “minutes per visit,” and resulting total/mean distinct. Any formula should be derived from the case or transparently mapped to those objects. No dependency on prior formal statistics, conditional-probability notation, or a long prerequisites section.
5. **Boundary and wrong model.** Avoid the false general rule “subgroups always matter more than the overall average” or “always average the two subgroup means.” The appropriate weights depend on the question and the target visit mix. Historical subgroup averages are estimates for planning, not causal guarantees or predictions immune to change. A lengthy caveat is unnecessary; an accurate statement of what is being compared suffices.
6. **Fresh check and feedback.** The check should require using a new mixture or evaluating a new aggregation, not merely recalling that A wins. Feedback must expose weighting, so a correct café with the wrong reason can be distinguished from understanding. Example evaluator probe, if needed after candidate freezing: if 25% of future visits are busy, expected means are A 4 and B 5.25. “A wins because A’s last-week overall average is lower” is a wrong reason despite the correct choice.

Critical failures: choosing B for the given 50/50 schedule solely from aggregate history; claiming A’s historical overall mean is lower; treating 9.2 and 3.9 as arithmetic errors; asserting universal group-mean averaging; using an unexplained formula as the only bridge.

## Prompt 2 — six dimensions

1. **Purpose and decision.** The learner should understand how to decide what can responsibly be said and what further check is worth seeking. The current evidence does not settle the venue. Good instruction can recommend checking current event information from an organizer and, meanwhile, distinguishing an unconfirmed report from a confirmed move. It should not send any actual message or claim to have checked the fictional event.
2. **Hard bridge: agreement and shared origin.** Three forwarded screenshots are three routes carrying one assertion, not three independent confirmations of its truth. A small origin-to-forwards sketch or equivalent causal explanation should let the learner trace this. “Check multiple sources” or “use trusted sources” without explaining why the source chain matters earns at most 1.
3. **Independent does not mean decisive.** Equipment entering the hall is a separate observation, but supports several explanations: storage, preparation, another event, a backup plan, or a real move. A good lesson distinguishes what was directly seen from the inferred event claim. It need not enumerate these exact alternatives. Treating that observation as conclusive venue confirmation fails.
4. **Useful next check with provenance.** Seek evidence specific to this event, tomorrow’s date, and the current venue decision, preferably a direct organizer communication or maintained event notice. Explain why that check answers the uncertainty instead of merely increasing the number of agreeing messages. Authority alone should not erase relevance, currency, or authenticity; these can be handled briefly in ordinary language.
5. **Transfer without a recipe slogan.** A new case should vary the pattern enough to reveal whether the learner reasons about shared origin and evidence relevance. For example, two independently written summaries can still derive from one unverified original; conversely, a direct updated event notice may be more informative than many reposts. Do not require a particular scenario or vocabulary. The explanation should avoid an absolute “one original source only counts once for every claim”: forwards can corroborate circulation, and independent sightings can corroborate existence of a notice without settling the notice’s truth.
6. **Feedback and novice load.** Feedback should identify the mistaken inference, not just pronounce the source reliable/unreliable. The learner should be able to draw a short evidence chain or explain “what this establishes/what remains uncertain.” Avoid jargon-heavy discussions of Bayesian dependence, epistemology, or a broad checklist that obscures this specific mechanism.

Critical failures: counting three forwards as three independent confirmations; saying agreement itself verifies truth; treating equipment movement as decisive; asserting anything forwarded is false; sending an announcement or pretending to browse fictional evidence.

## Cross-test protocol expectations

The task requests small lessons. Any skill invocation should scale to this scope: no website, app, 64-slide deck, mandatory multi-document learner-facing process, or claim that the full masterclass pipeline is indispensable. Short internal planning is compatible. Critique excessive formalism if it consumes learner attention or materially evades the task, rather than penalizing internal notes merely for existing.

The two outputs should demonstrate different appropriate representations and feedback, not mechanically instantiate one analogy or a fixed interaction ritual. A clear static worked example is acceptable; gratuitous interaction is not necessary. Both outputs should remain under 650 words and avoid unsupported claims about mastery, durable retention, or superiority over expert teaching.

Record candidate model/context, skill revision, whether authoring history or the key was shared, and any later revisions. A fresh-context candidate remains an AI-generated artifact. Its apparent success is evidence of protocol applicability on two unseen tasks, not actual learner learning or proof that the original PCA course is repaired.
