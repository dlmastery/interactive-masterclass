# Round 2 — educational cross-critique

This review is now informed by `proposed-process.txt`, both other round-1 reports, and my original artifact inspection. It is no longer blind. Agreement among these reports is triangulation of reasons, not independent replication or evidence of human learning.

## Accepted, with reasons

**Keep purpose, inference, and evidence as separate obligations.** The proposal correctly distinguishes software behavior, explanatory judgment, simulated performance, human learning, preference, and retention. This directly addresses the unsupported promotion from a working interactive artifact to a successful lesson. Add a purpose-to-method link: what makes this representation, approximation, or criterion useful for the learner’s actual objective? A motivating story cannot justify an arbitrary later objective.

**Pilot the bottleneck as well as the opening.** A clear introduction cannot validate the derivative, proof, or causal bridge that carries the course. The process and comparator reviews support testing a difficult transition before multiplying its format. The pilot must include its immediate prerequisites and next use; an isolated polished panel can hide a broken transition.

**Allow direct explanation and fade support.** This protects novices from a mandatory prediction ritual when they lack a model from which to predict. Ask an answerable prediction, give a worked case, or diagnose a prerequisite. Do not infer preparation from age. Separate learning to read a representation from reasoning with it.

**Keep narrow revision binding and bounded repair.** Reopening evidence affected by a changed example or representation is useful. Preserve disagreements, uncertainty, and untested outcomes. Two failed causal repairs should prompt a new hypothesis, although a suspected rendering defect and an instructional failure must not silently share an attempt counter.

## Reject or narrow

**Reject three fully populated graphs as the default minimum.** The three views have different jobs, but need not become three maintained artifacts for every short lesson. Require one compact inference route. Generate dependency and learner-state views only where changed artifacts or actual branches make them useful. A five-minute explanation should not require a schema project.

**Reject one record per sentence or routine prerequisite.** Even the proposed critical-inference record has eleven fields. Use a concise paragraph or table row for the few bottlenecks, with optional fields. Mandatory completeness encourages generic text and review fatigue. Evaluate the quality of the explanatory witness, not the count of filled fields.

**Narrow “freeze only after evidence.”** Evidence cannot always include available humans or delayed follow-up. Permit a provisional route after a sufficiently inspected pilot, label its evidence level, and distinguish expansion authorization from demonstrated learning. Otherwise the process either stalls or quietly renames AI approval as learner evidence.

**Narrow paired comparison.** Matching goals, prior knowledge, and time improves interpretation; it does not make a same-model judge an independent novice. Random labels can hide authorship while writing style, context, and known evaluation criteria still reveal it. Record what was hidden and what was shared. A creator-blind preference comparison is not a controlled learning comparison.

**Reject a schema pass as a proxy for execution quality, even indirectly.** Revision hashes and blocked delivery can expose stale receipts. They cannot verify that the author actually diagnosed the learner, that a visual explains a mechanism, or that its rubric rewards the right inference. Keep the tool’s output narrowly named: current records, unresolved findings, and unknown evidence.

## Concrete generic graph example

For a short lesson on binary search, the outcome is “find a catalog entry efficiently and explain when halving the search is valid.”

`Need: too many entries to inspect → current rule: skip half after comparing the middle → discriminating pair: sorted versus scrambled catalog → bridge: order makes a comparison rule out an entire interval → representation: mark the interval still capable of containing the target → justification: each discard preserves every possible target → new task: target absent, with an explanation of when search can stop.`

The prerequisite edge is “can compare ordered labels” before the bridge. A learner who reads the display backward receives one worked interval and returns to the same search state. A correct guessed location with an invalid discard reason does not satisfy the exit. If the catalog example changes, recheck the comparison and transfer claims; an unrelated font correction need not reopen the entire route. The learner sees a catalog problem, not this graph.

## Strongest unresolved risk and falsification

The strongest risk is a self-confirming author–reviewer system that manufactures persuasive teaching rationales. The protocol may optimize explanations of its own compliance while leaving learning unchanged. A separate held-out task helps, but the same model family can share blind spots and solve from prior knowledge rather than the lesson.

Pre-register a small comparison: authors use either this protocol or a compact baseline requiring purpose, worked example, misconception, and transfer. Match topic, preparation, time, and output budget. Unfamiliar target learners attempt an unaided explanation and structurally new problem; record guidance and navigation burden. If the expanded protocol adds substantial authoring cost without improving bottleneck understanding or transfer, remove its extra machinery. If its AI-passed lessons repeatedly fail those learner tasks, revise the review criteria rather than lowering the threshold. This test can disconfirm the protocol’s added value; passing it would still not establish universal superiority.
