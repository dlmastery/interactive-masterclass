Concrete places where the route still asks too much of the learner

The fresh reviewer identified the move from direction to cosine/sine, dot product, and matrix notation around slides 13–15. Arithmetic can be visible while the reason a weighted sum measures position on a tilted ruler remains hidden. “Projection” names the bridge; it does not cross it.

The route motivates reconstruction loss and soon asserts its equivalence with retained spread, while the supporting argument is deferred until slides 51–52. A learner can therefore optimize one quantity for many screens on trust after being motivated by another. The issue is not that every theorem must appear immediately. It is that the chosen objective needs sufficient justification when it begins to carry the explanation.

The stationarity material exposes several ideas together—tangent, gradient, directional derivative, and orders of change—before each is fully supported. The nonzero-null-vector to singular-matrix to zero-determinant transition and the later SVD step also deserve explicit prerequisite accounting. Additional screens help only if they resolve these actual inferences.

What the diagnosis does not imply

It does not imply that rigorous mathematics should be removed, or that a 20-year-old needs a childish explanation. It does not imply that all controls are useless, that the original version met the later depth requirement, or that every technical problem was imaginary. The first compact version appears to have had advantages in framing and screen economy worth preserving. The later version has useful stable examples, covariance contrasts, global-optimality distinctions, and limit cases. Neither fact proves a complete successful lesson.

A serious rival explanation is that the content could work with skilled live narration, while its self-paced presentation fails to manage attention and timing. Another is a mismatch between assumed readiness and this learner’s expectations. These possibilities should be tested; they do not excuse claiming self-contained teaching quality without evidence.

The token cost cannot be reconstructed exactly from the available records. The visible cost drivers were duplicated research/process text, late framework troubleshooting, broad rebuilds before a discriminating pilot, local repairs without strategic stopping, and reviews that reassured more than they falsified. I should not invent a billing total or attribute every cost to one cause.

Interactive software has a potential advantage: learners can make a prediction, change a meaningful condition, inspect a counterexample, receive specific feedback, and return to their own work. What the learning research supports—and does not

Mayer and Anderson (1992) studied multimedia explanations of mechanical systems. Their transfer results support testing alignment of explanation and depicted operation under the studied conditions. They do not establish that more animation always improves teaching or that an effect size transfers to this course.

Alfieri and colleagues (2011) synthesized discovery-learning studies. The distinction between unsupported discovery and guided activity matters. Our design implication is to use examples, explanations, hints, and scaffolding when the learner needs them, then fade support. “Super interactive” does not mean asking a novice to rediscover missing theory.

Your supplied guidance’s retrieval figures match Roediger and Karpicke (2006), Experiment 2: repeated study yielded 83% recall after five minutes and 40% after one week; study plus repeated recall yielded 71% and 61%. These were separate delay groups learning prose, with different study and recall period durations. They are not universal forgetting rates, equal-time efficiency estimates, evidence of an AI advantage, or proof that a twelve-role workflow produces mathematical intuition. Retrieval can strengthen and test an acquired model; it cannot substitute for first explaining that model.

4. The goal and the evidence needed to claim it

The generic goal is to help a specified learner acquire an appropriate usable structure: a mechanism, mathematical model, argument, decision rule, classification, or procedure. They should be able to use it beyond the demonstrated example. The route should feel motivated and coherent, while preserving the depth promised for the subject.

  1. Purpose: explain what problem the idea solves and what learning it enables.
  2. Model: reconstruct the main relation and predict a meaningful change.
  3. Justification: explain why the method works, including requested derivation or evidence.
  4. Discrimination: identify when a plausible competing rule fails.
  5. Transfer: solve a changed problem without copying the example or receiving its answer.
  6. Retention: reconstruct and use the idea later, when that has actually been tested.

These are configurable outcomes, not six mandatory quizzes on every slide. A short answer may serve only one. A masterclass needs a connected selection appropriate to its promise.

“Beat the best YouTube tutorial” must therefore be tested against a bounded comparator: same learner preparation, goal, depth, time, and assessment. Include navigation overhead and allow video replay. Separate preference from performance. A short pilot can reveal a failure; it cannot prove broad superiority. Same-topic sequential viewing has carryover even when order is counterbalanced. Appropriate human comparison may require separate groups or carefully matched tasks and blind scoring.

5. The outer loop, inner loop, and student loop

Outer loop: select and revise the teaching strategy

Purpose + readiness + promised capability
    → inspect contrasting explanations and subject evidence
    → two serious candidate routes
    → strongest objection and a test that could resolve it
    → pilot opening + bottleneck + unfamiliar application
    → compare with the stated goal and relevant expert segment
    → keep provisionally / revise / abandon the route
    → expand only at the evidence level actually earned

The unit of change here is the route, example, representation, or objective. If the learner still cannot see why the method solves the problem, adding more screens is not the default next action. A route can be provisionally chosen after a strong explanation review without pretending human efficacy is established. An explicit user review requirement remains a separate stop.

Inner loop: earn and verify one difficult inference

Identify the learner question and likely wrong rule
    → choose a discriminating case or worked explanation
    → show the relevant operation and invariant
    → map it to the new representation or formal step
    → ask for a new prediction, explanation, or application
    → inspect the answer AND its visible warrant
    → one causal repair
    → recheck the affected obligation

The unit of change is a specific bridge. A repair hypothesis states what was missing and what observation should improve. A failed bridge can be repaired by a better example, direct explanation, contrast, notation mapping, scaffold, or sequencing change. It need not require another feature. After two unsuccessful causal repairs to the same inference, the default is to reopen strategy rather than repeat the pattern. This is an effort guardrail, not a scientific law of learning; unrelated rendering repairs have different issue records.

Student runtime loop: respond to the learner in front of the tutor

Observe readiness or label an assumption
    → explain/model as needed
    → learner attempt
    → interpret reasoning with uncertainty
    → specific feedback or bounded detour
    → less-assisted retry
    → unfamiliar application
    → later retrieval / transfer when in scope

This loop needs application support if it is to run live. It preserves the current problem, question, visual state, attempt, assistance, pending action, and return point. A correct answer with a wrong reason follows a different path from demonstrated understanding. An ambiguous answer is not automatically wrong. A stuck learner receives a smaller task or worked step rather than endless Socratic questioning. A skipped check permits navigation without awarding mastery.

An authored slide course can implement limited branches. A general tutor that interprets free-form student feedback requires additional model and state integration. The revised skill describes that distinction; it does not claim to have built that separate application.

A generic worked planning example: binary search

Need: find a catalog entry without inspecting every item
 → naive shortcut: compare the middle and discard half
 → contrast: sorted catalog versus scrambled catalog
 → bridge: ordering makes one comparison rule out an interval
 → representation: the interval that can still contain the target
 → justification: every discard preserves all possible locations
 → fresh case: the target is absent; explain when search stops

The required entry knowledge is comparing ordered labels. If the learner reads the interval backward, a worked interval can supply that prerequisite and return them to the same task. A lucky correct location with an invalid discard reason does not meet the exit condition. Changing the catalog representation reopens the bridge and transfer checks; changing an unrelated footer need not reopen source research.

The smallest useful bottleneck record

For a few critical transitions, record: the need and entering model; target capability; explanatory witness; mapping and validity conditions; learner work versus supplied work; likely wrong rule; and exit task with feedback and next use. Use a concise paragraph or table row. Requiring eleven fields for every sentence would encourage generic filler and review fatigue.

For mathematical derivations, track definition versus identity versus theorem, assumptions, units, and necessary versus sufficient conditions. For other subjects, track causal alternatives, interpretation, warrants, and limits. The generic discipline is to earn an inference, not to force every topic into PCA-like geometry or calculus.

8. What the revised harness enforces—and what it cannot

The original script was an evidence journal. It checked that files existed, hashes matched, and earlier linear gates had passes. The revised guardrail checks a compact contract, named obligations, typed receipts, registered artifact revisions, dependencies, findings, and attempt history. It is not an autonomous tutor, a proof of truthful execution, or a security boundary around agent tools.

A receipt identifies the reviewed obligation, claim/review type, scenario, observed result, limitations, reviewer relationship, and evidence files. The recorded revision includes declared inputs and dependency state. A critical unresolved finding prevents affected acceptance. A changed declared input invalidates dependent receipts while unrelated branches remain usable. Unregistered inputs remain outside the freshness guarantee; the coordinator must maintain a complete relevant manifest.

Two failed repairs of the same teaching issue require a new strategy hypothesis and a new pilot before the workflow can continue as if the problem were solved. Simply renaming the finding is not a valid reset. Legacy records remain distinguishable and are not silently promoted into stronger claims. Missing human evidence remains unverified; a truthful reviewed candidate can still be delivered without pretending a learner study occurred.

The boundary is explicit: the script can reject malformed or disallowed recorded transitions. It cannot prevent someone from directly editing a file, verify that a report is truthful, prove a reviewer is independent, judge the adequacy of an explanation, or establish learning. The coordinator must use it honestly. The strongest remaining systemic risk is persuasive self-attestation by correlated author and reviewer models.

How to challenge the process itself

Compare this process with a compact baseline that requires only purpose, a worked example, a misconception, and transfer. Match topic, preparation, authoring allowance, and learner time. Have unfamiliar target learners explain and solve a structurally new problem. If the expanded process adds cost without improving bottleneck understanding or transfer, remove the extra machinery. If AI-passed lessons repeatedly fail learners, revise the review criteria instead of lowering the threshold.

9. Cost control without pretending the task is complete

Declare work limits before production. A useful default is two route candidates, one pilot batch, and no more than two failed causal repairs per unchanged inference before a strategy decision. These are adjustable planning limits, not a user token budget or universal teaching laws. Record a reason for changing a limit; do not silently keep expanding it.

Reserve effort for the bottleneck, requested proof or central argument, and verification before decorative polish or prerequisite expansion. Reuse source notes and reviews still valid for the same claim and revision. Repeat a review only after relevant change or a live unresolved disagreement. A human study is not an automatic dependency for every draft; it is necessary for the corresponding efficacy claim.

At a limit, preserve the draft and report the precise failure, the attempted hypotheses, remaining uncertainty, and the smallest next test. Do not label a capped run complete merely because effort was spent. Do not ask the user to solve routine implementation decisions. Here, however, you explicitly own the review before the next build, so that future build remains outside this task.
