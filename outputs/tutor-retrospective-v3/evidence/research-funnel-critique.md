# A broad candidate funnel without pretending breadth is depth

This is an author-informed process critique, based on the accompanying 31-video discovery run. It makes no claim of independent review or learner gains. It does not change the course or the installed skill.

The earlier rule to inspect a few contrasting explainers was too easy to satisfy by using the user's examples as the complete universe. It allowed selection to occur before real discovery. For this user's substantial masterclass request, a minimum of 30 relevant candidates is a concrete breadth requirement. Three exemplars can be a result of screening a larger pool; they cannot silently replace that pool. A brief factual answer should still bypass this pipeline.

**Use two distinct inventories.** The candidate ledger records identity, advertised scope, audience assumptions, reach data and access depth. The technique ledger records things actually inspected: a motivating question, example, invariant, contrast, representation mapping, formal argument, misconception treatment, or failure case, with timestamps and source modality. A search result does not become a technique observation because an agent writes a persuasive summary.

**Search by unanswered teaching obligations, not only famous names.** Combine broad topic searches with purpose, geometry/mechanism, proof/evidence, implementation, applications, misconceptions, limits and competing formulations. Add high-reach anchors, small-reach candidates with distinctive advertised routes, university/creator materials and a recency pass. Record missing language or accessibility coverage. Canonicalize video IDs; distinguish related series and adapted source lineages from independent approaches. Do not pad the count with general prerequisites, reuploads, unrelated acronyms or clips with no additional relevant scope.

**Use popularity in discovery, not as a learning score.** This run observed 29 view counts and 26 like counts through indexed YouTube metadata. Their underlying snapshot dates were not available. Two creators remained unverified. The ledger preserves those limits. High views may identify a widely encountered explanation; they cannot certify accuracy, prerequisite fit, conceptual transfer, or superiority. Low counts do not prove hidden quality. There was no formal rating data.

**Narrow for information gained.** The proposed next pass is ten varied candidates, each with a specific uncertainty to resolve, followed by a smaller set of complementary techniques and two competing routes. These are planning allocations, not universal scientific thresholds. For example, inspect whether a geometric sweep actually justifies a maximum, whether a dataset earns the objective, and whether a practical comparison explains its conditions. Include openings and surrounding context so a clipped proof is not judged without its prerequisites. Inspect pixels and timing before judging animation; a transcript supports wording and sequence only. Preserve failed access and replace inaccessible candidates only with an explicit reason.

**Stop for a reason.** Thirty entries alone are not sufficient if every entry repeats one route. Conversely, fully transcribing 30 videos is not required to revise a skill. After breadth is met, stop additional discovery when the important route uncertainties have plausible, inspectable alternatives; deepen only where a decision depends on it. The resulting pilot should test the chosen explanation, not merely display the research count.

**Keep the benchmark honest.** A candidate pool plus expert-inspired techniques can improve the design hypothesis. It cannot establish that students learned, retained, or transferred more than from an expert video. Match goals, preparation and learner time for any later comparison, record navigation cost, and separate artifact review from observed human outcomes. The transferable method is broad discovery followed by evidence-bearing selection, not a PCA-specific camera story, covariance sequence, or fixed number of animations.
