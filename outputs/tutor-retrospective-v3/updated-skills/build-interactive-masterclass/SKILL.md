# Build an exceptional explainer

Aim for the strongest explanation available for the stated audience and promise: lasting intuition, rigorous understanding, sound decisions, independent application, and engaging delivery. Quality has priority over authoring speed, slide count, or implementation simplicity. The user's ambition is a leading public explainer; ratings, reach, and millions of views are goals to investigate, never guaranteed outcomes.

This is canonical teaching protocol version 3. `build-intuition-masterclass` is a compatible entrypoint. `build-research-codelabs` retains its execution/science requirements and reuses this teaching standard. Do not run duplicate research pipelines.

## Scope, audience, and quality

Recover purpose, audience, prior knowledge, output modes, depth, constraints, prior feedback, and useful existing work. Age is context, not demonstrated preparation. Distinguish authoring time from learner time. A complex subject may need 100-plus slides, extensive notes, experiments, and many revisions. Do not impose an arbitrary duration or size cap; organize the experience into coherent chapters and routes.

Keep explicit user/resource/tool limits. Quality-first does not authorize purchases, publishing, background scheduling, or unrelated projects. A retrospective/skill update does not authorize rebuilding the course. A plan-only request stops before implementation. Small questions and edits receive proportionate direct work.

## End-to-end working route

1. **Earn the purpose.** State why learn this, the problem or puzzle, what the learner gains, and where the idea fits. Define observable capabilities and transfer before choosing screens.
2. **Cover the whole promise.** Map what/why/when/where/how, necessary foundations, formal justification, worked examples, applications and modern uses, assumptions, limitations, diagnostics, mitigations, alternatives, best practices, misconceptions, and transfer. Mark justified exclusions explicitly. Weave these into the route rather than append disconnected lists.
3. **Survey broadly.** For flagship work discover at least 30 unique relevant video candidates where available, including popular/well-received sources and distinctive rigorous approaches. The user's examples are seeds. Record actual metadata and access depth; missing views/ratings remain unknown. Screen substantive material and deeply inspect complementary strong approaches, including actual visual/oral segments for visual/oral claims. Expand research when coverage or pilots expose gaps. Do not mistake 30 URLs for 30 understood lessons.
4. **Verify and synthesize.** Check technical/current claims against primary sources. Compare serious competing teaching routes. Preserve their strongest objections. Build an original, coherent narrative with stable notation and representations; do not assemble a collage of copied best moments.
5. **Design the inference graph.** Connect learner need to mechanism, justification, application, failure, mitigation, and choice among alternatives. Insert background where it unlocks the next step. Reserve the central derivation and practical judgment; do not spend all attention on preparation.
6. **Pilot the explanatory burden.** Build an opening, the hardest bridge with immediate context, and a decision/transfer scene. Probe the actual delivery medium early. For video, also test a narrated sequence with synchronized visuals. Compare the pilot with strong reference explanations and the stated learner promise.
7. **Expand and refine.** Continue until the authorized scope and required quality checks are satisfied. More rounds are appropriate when they improve the explanation. After repeated failure, change the hypothesis, example, representation, sequence, or reviewer; do not repeat unchanged polish. Do not use a retry count as permission to deliver known slop.
8. **Review the complete experience.** Check correctness, breadth, conceptual continuity, transfer, limitations/alternatives, meaningful interaction, visual attention, oration/notes, language, accessibility, and real runtime separately. Trace the whole route after local repairs. Preserve objections and verify fixes on the current revision.
9. **Deliver a reviewable package.** Include the requested course/deck, source, illustrations, narration/notes, launch/navigation paths, research ledger, coverage/decision map, evidence, and precise remaining limits. Publish only when authorized. If changing the skill, provide exact before/after diffs, rationale, tests, and a versioned handoff.

## Three distinct improvement loops

**External authoring strategy:** learner contract → broad landscape → competing routes → critical pilot → comparative/cold review → strategic revision → full-course review. Its unit of change is the explanatory approach.

**Internal explanation repair:** one failed inference → likely wrong model → discriminating example or direct explanation → learner-visible attempt → specific feedback/evidence → causal repair → recheck. Its unit is one bridge, not the whole theme.

**Learner runtime:** readiness → model/scaffold as needed → attempt and reasoning → contingent help → less-assisted retry → new context → later reconstruction when in scope. Its unit is the learner's current knowledge state. A static course can supply authored branches; it is not automatically a general adaptive tutor.

For tutoring systems, also distinguish the practice-selection outer loop from the step-feedback inner loop. Read adaptive-tutoring.md for how they connect without confusing learner adaptation with authoring revision.

## Non-negotiable evidence boundaries

A control must help the learner test, inspect, construct, discriminate, or explain. Software doing arithmetic is not learner reasoning. An equation must be connected to the object/operation and why it serves the purpose. A limitation needs its mechanism and response, not a disclaimer at the end.

Use fresh-context reviewers where possible, with learner-facing material and neutral tasks before author diagnoses. Record prior authorship/shared context. AI knowledge can conceal a bad lesson; require visible warrants and held-out tasks. Agreement is not proof.

Keep software verification, explanation review, simulated tasks, human learning, comparative preference, delayed retention, and audience reach separate. The CLI checks registered records, not truth or learning. No claim of universal superiority or virality follows from an AI rubric.

Use clear, natural, precise language. Preserve this user's 80% ASD-STE100-compatible prose target with an honest full-coverage audit; sentence length alone does not prove it. Spoken mathematics also needs words, pauses, and visual referents, not just symbol reading.

Record durable decisions and next actions for long work. Preserve user review dependencies. Stop only for achieved scope, a real external limit, user direction, or a genuine missing prerequisite to continue—not because an arbitrary slide or effort target was reached.
