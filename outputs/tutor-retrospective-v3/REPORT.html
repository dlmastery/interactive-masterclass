1. What I missed in the last retrospective

I narrowed your desired outcome to “explain the mechanism well.” That is necessary but incomplete. An excellent learner should also know why the idea exists, when it is a good choice, where it fits, how it is used now, how it fails, what can be done about that failure, and which alternative may be better. Those are part of understanding the subject, not optional decorations after the proof.

I also used your examples too literally. A few admired creators should have started a broad search. The earlier skill allowed a small, familiar source set to become the whole research universe before genuine discovery. The new protocol has a concrete breadth requirement and a separate deep-inspection requirement. Neither can masquerade as the other.

Finally, the earlier cost-control language was stronger than your actual preference. Avoiding repeated uninformative work remains sensible, but an internally invented time or attempt ceiling must not force delivery of weak teaching. I have removed that interpretation. Repeated failure now means changing the explanatory strategy and testing again; it does not mean declaring the output good enough.

The prior root cause still applies: implementation success and persuasive review language do not establish learning. Version 3 adds the missing substance and delivery standards while preserving that evidence boundary. The earlier retrospective remains available as the historical diagnosis; this report supersedes its narrower research and authoring-limit guidance.

2. The complete subject contract

The new content contract contains 17 coverage dimensions. Each maps to an actual learner question, scene or exercise, explanatory evidence, source where needed, and review status. A heading alone does not satisfy a dimension. Narrow tasks may have justified exclusions; a full masterclass cannot use that exemption to omit its promise.

Coverage groupWhat must become understandableWhat would fail review
Purpose and identityWhy learn this, what the learner gains, what the idea is and is not, why it was needed.A definition or impressive application name without an earned reason to care.
When and whereConditions for use, conditions against use, its place in a workflow or discipline, inputs and consequences.A universally recommended technique with no suitability judgment.
Mechanism and rigorReconstructable intuition, foundations at use, complete worked case, requested proof or evidential argument.A correct formula with the crucial conceptual connection left to the learner.
Applications and current practiceA real decision, why this method serves it, evidence of current use, the success criterion, and the boundary of a toy example.Lists of industries; a prototype described as widespread deployment; stale “state of the art” claims.
Limitations and responsesAssumption, failure mechanism, diagnostic, mitigation, cost and residual weakness, alternative or non-use decision.A disclaimer list or a mitigation described as a cure without evidence.
Alternatives and best practicesStrong competing approaches and simple baselines compared on relevant criteria; practices with rationale, trigger, check, and exception.Weak straw-man alternatives or context-free tips.
Misconceptions, transfer, retentionDiscriminating cases, independent use and rejection of the method, adaptation to new contexts, later reconstruction where tested.Copied answers, assisted completion presented as mastery, or unmeasured retention claims.

These dimensions should create a natural flow. A useful application creates the need; the mechanism explains the effect; the justification establishes its conditions; a failure exposes a limitation; a diagnostic identifies it; a mitigation helps at a cost; an alternative resolves a different need. The course becomes a connected argument rather than a larger checklist.

Need and gain → method → justification → application

The contract adapts across subjects. Mathematics needs definitions, invariants, and proof; empirical science needs observation and uncertainty; history needs provenance and competing interpretations; craft needs performance cues and recovery; ethics needs values, stakeholders, and warranted arguments. “Mitigation” in history may mean narrowing a claim or triangulating sources, not repairing the past. The architecture is reusable; the evidence and narrative must change with the topic.

3. Broad discovery followed by real understanding

For a flagship explainer, the revised rule is to discover at least 30 unique relevant video candidates where available. Search intuition, visual reasoning, derivation, worked examples, implementation, applications, limits, alternatives, university teaching, and practitioners. Your named examples are seeds. Popular and well-received material deserves attention, alongside distinctive rigorous sources with smaller audiences.

Popularity metadata must be observed and dated. Views and likes are not formal ratings, nor evidence that a novice learned. The ledger cannot honestly claim “globally top 30” from a bounded search. Reuploads, repeated clips, unrelated meanings of an acronym, and general prerequisite videos must not pad the topic count.

A diverse deep shortlist of approximately 8–12 can be a starting allocation, not a ceiling or automatic pass. Research expands when the key proof, modern use, failure response, or alternative remains unsupported. The learner’s obligations determine what needs deep inspection. A literal collage of the most appealing moments from 30 teachers could be less coherent than any one of them.

Source scarcity and access failure are different. Thirty identified relevant videos can satisfy discovery even if some cannot be played; content review remains incomplete. If broad, documented searches genuinely find only nine relevant candidates, report nine and the gap, then use additional authoritative formats without relabeling them as videos. A small factual answer does not trigger this flagship workflow.

4. Human and AI tutoring strategy

The new tutoring reference draws on seven inspected primary or official sources in this round, plus the earlier retrieval source. It distinguishes experimental results, design frameworks, and our proposed synthesis. It does not call the combined protocol a validated universal method.

Evidence or frameworkAction it informsBoundary
IES practice guideCombine worked examples and practice; connect concrete and abstract representations; ask explanatory questions with sufficient preparation.Different recommendations have different support; no universal AI interaction rhythm follows.
Cognitive apprenticeshipMake expert recognition, choice, checking, and recovery visible; coach and fade support.A design framework, not proof that a skill file reproduces expert judgment.
Formative assessment and ICAPLet observed reasoning change the next instructional action; inspect cognitive work rather than clicks.Quiz presence and UI interaction do not establish responsive or constructive learning.
Kestin et al., 2025Use verified materials and reliable sequence control in an AI tutor.Immediate physics results from a structured package do not establish all-subject superiority or retention.

The practical standard is to model, diagnose, coach, give specific feedback, fade assistance, and test transfer. A learner who cannot predict may need a worked example first. A correct answer with an invalid reason needs a discriminating case. Ambiguous reasoning should prompt a diagnostic question. A valid alternative method should not be rejected because it differs from the expected wording.

Reviewer disagreement and disposition: the research memo recommended letting uncertainty, rather than a mandatory source count, control discovery. That recommendation was not adopted as written because you explicitly require a broad landscape of at least 30 relevant candidates where available. We retained its warning that count alone does not establish quality, while preserving your floor and requiring deeper inspection. The original memo remains unchanged as evidence of the debate.

The AI-specific layer adds checked reference solutions, current problem state, verified dynamic values, uncertainty handling, tested response branches, and explicit separation of task completion from competence. A human tutor can select or reject suggestions. A static course must disclose the boundaries of its authored feedback. The full research note records inspected sections and study limitations.

The learning graph gains explicit links for applies in, fails when, detected by, mitigated by, trades off with, and alternative to. Modern application nodes link to dated evidence. Narration, visual state, assessment, and source revision share scene IDs. Changing a worked case can therefore reopen the spoken explanation and feedback as well as the formula.

One compact project record can hold these views. We do not require three separate graph documents for every small lesson. The learner sees a clear route of questions and a useful location marker. Internal dependencies and review records remain an authoring tool.

6. Oration and speaker notes are now part of the product

The new production reference specifies exact narration, an event-cued storyboard, deliberate pointing, pauses, transitions, mathematical speech, rough animatics, actual rehearsal, channel-specific reviews, captions, and delivery checks. “Explain this diagram” is not sufficient speaker notes.

For each cue a second presenter should know what to say, what is visible before and after, where to point, what action or reveal to trigger, when to pause, what response to expect, how to handle a likely misunderstanding, and why the next scene follows. Production directions are separate from spoken prose. Timing starts as an estimate and becomes measured only after rehearsal.

SAY: “Both copies came from the same original report.
If that report is wrong, both copies can repeat its error.”
SHOW: One source node feeding two branches.
POINT: Original source, then the branches.
PAUSE: Let the viewer trace the shared origin.
ASK: “Would one more copy settle the claim?”
NEXT: Contrast a copied assertion with independent event evidence.

This is an illustrative note pattern, not a performed or learner-tested scene. The skill requires more than reading symbols aloud: explain what a quantity measures, where each term comes from, why the operation helps, and what conditions make the claim valid. Technical pacing cannot be reduced to one words-per-minute target.

Linear video and interactive slides need distinct delivery paths. A video demonstrates chosen inputs and can invite a pause; it cannot react to an unseen viewer answer. An interactive tutor must respond to actual selected state, stop stale speech after interruption, and restore a pending question after a detour. Exporting notebook slides does not automatically produce a finished video.

The language audit now includes spoken notes, captions, and transcripts, with explicit denominators and duplicate handling. The 80% ASD-STE100 target remains unverified without the appropriate full audit; short sentences alone are insufficient. Accessibility planning follows relevant W3C guidance, with actual review still required.

7. Quality first, with honest publication goals

The revised skills do not cap a course at 40, 50, 64, or 100 slides. They do not stop because an internal research allocation expired. They do preserve actual user, tool, compute, and experimental-evaluation constraints. In a codelab, fair experiment budgets and laptop-sized learner activities serve scientific validity and accessibility; they are distinct from rushing the authoring process.

Two failed causal repairs still trigger a strategy change and fresh pilot. That safeguard prevents repeating the same unproductive action; it permits continued improvement afterward. The codelab’s old generic three-image-output ceiling was also replaced: repeated errors require a new specification or representation, and an incorrect figure must not be selected merely because an attempt count was reached.

The finish criterion is that the authorized content and production obligations pass their actual checks, critical findings are resolved, and remaining evidence limits are explicit. Work continues while a material gap has a useful next test. Universal perfection cannot be established by an AI panel, and repeated unchanged review is not a quality improvement.

The ambition for top ratings and millions of views is retained. It is not a guarantee. YouTube’s official guidance describes personalized relevance and long-term satisfaction. Reach also depends on audience, topic demand, competition, and distribution. The skills distinguish production quality, learning outcomes, and public response.

After authorized publication, retention, impressions, CTR, relevant comments, and platform-supported packaging tests can inform hypotheses. A retention spike can reflect confusion and replay; watch-time share is not a learning score. Necessary reasoning should not be removed just to improve a viewing metric. The publication-feedback loop is designed, not automatically scheduled or executed.
