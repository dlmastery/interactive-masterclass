# Quality-first preparation, execution, and external review

Read for a substantial course. This is an execution guide, not proof that a learner will succeed.

## The acceptance contract

Record audience/readiness, purpose, promised capabilities, media, required depth, complete coverage map, current-source needs, language/accessibility requirements, and explicit resource constraints. Identify what counts as a critical failure: false central claim, unsupported key inference, missing requested coverage, misleading figure, unusable interaction, or a narration/visual mismatch that breaks understanding.

Quality takes priority over fast generation and small outputs. Authoring may need many sessions or a day of work. Do not invent a deadline, slide ceiling, source ceiling, or token budget. A user-specified resource boundary still applies; save a clear checkpoint if execution cannot continue. Long work needs compact durable state, not repeated rediscovery.

## Outer authoring loop

1. Recover the brief, useful baseline, failures, and acceptance evidence.
2. Build the broad research landscape and subject reference set.
3. Develop competing routes with full coverage, bottleneck, strongest objection, and falsifying pilot.
4. Choose an initial route provisionally and record why.
5. Implement representative purpose, mechanism/proof, application/limit, and narrated/interactive scenes.
6. Obtain separate initial reviews before cross-critique where feasible.
7. Decide which objection requires local repair and which invalidates the route.
8. Revise and retest; expand supported patterns.
9. Walk the complete course cold, including transitions and promised payoff.
10. Reopen the relevant decision when new evidence or user feedback warrants it.

A pilot is a risk-reduction step, not a substitute for the complete authorized course. When the full build is authorized, continue through completion without inventing approval stops. Honor a user-requested review-before-build or review-before-publication dependency.

## Inner authoring loop

Use a stable finding ID and record observed learner-visible failure, affected capability, plausible cause, repair hypothesis, change, and discriminating recheck. Preserve unsuccessful attempts; do not reset history by changing filenames.

Distinguish:
- Content failure: claim, proof, or application wrong/incomplete.
- Explanatory failure: correct material lacks a bridge or purpose.
- Representation failure: diagram implies the wrong relation.
- Attention failure: the needed evidence is hidden, split, or rushed.
- Runtime failure: control/state/navigation does not work.
- Delivery failure: spoken script and visual state disagree.
- Assessment failure: the task can be passed by copying or a wrong rule.

Two unsuccessful causal repairs to the same teaching failure are a diagnostic trigger to reconsider the strategy. They are not a cap on total quality work. The existing CLI enforces a new strategy plus fresh pilot after that trigger; a successful new pilot permits continued work. Overall authoring continues within authorization until acceptance or a real blocking limit. If more unchanged attempts yield no new information, change the test/representation/reviewer rather than simply generating more.

## Quality reviews with distinct jobs

Assign roles according to the actual project; separate people/agents when available:
- Subject specialist: truth, scope, assumptions, current evidence, proof/argument.
- Educator: purpose, readiness, inference sequence, misconceptions, transfer.
- Practitioner: when/where, realistic applications, failures, mitigations, alternatives, best practices.
- Visual/oral editor: semantic fidelity, attention, readability, speaking, timing, event cues.
- Learner-interface reviewer: attempts, feedback, accessibility, navigation, notebook return.
- Independent evaluator: fresh tasks and strongest rival explanation.

A role label is not a credential. One reviewer can cover several jobs when suitable; six roles do not automatically mean six agents or six long documents. Preserve provenance and disagreement. A review that merely says “excellent” or supplies an average score cannot close a critical finding.

## The quality ladder

1. Source and coverage review: the promised subject is represented accurately and completely.
2. Critical bridge review: learner-visible warrants support the difficult steps.
3. Artifact review: visuals, script, notes, interactions, and assessments align.
4. Rehearsal/runtime review: actual performance or rendered interface matches the artifact.
5. Unfamiliar reviewer/learner pilot: observe reasoning and friction with no author coaching.
6. Comparative learning and delayed transfer: obtain suitable human evidence when those claims are in scope.
7. Publication feedback: evaluate reach, satisfaction, confusion, and corrections after authorized release.

Do not force unavailable human evidence into a fake pass. Deliver an accurately labeled reviewed candidate when authorized; describe what would be needed for stronger claims. A lack of human testing does not justify ignoring artifact defects.

## End-to-end records and dependencies

Use one compact project record with links to real outputs: user requirements, research inventory, coverage/inference map, route decisions, storyboard/notes, artifacts, findings, tested revisions, and next action. Keep source/evidence manifests complete enough that changing content invalidates affected reviews. Required breadth dimensions need scene/proof/application coverage, not just field names.

After a local repair, recheck affected outputs plus any global dependency: changing an example may change calculations, proof notation, speaker notes, illustrations, quiz feedback, and exported notes. Preserve valid unrelated work. Test a rendered final candidate, not only its authoring source.

A handoff states the exact accepted revision, open findings, scope, research access limits, what was genuinely tested, and what remains to do. Include decisions and rejected routes so the next model does not repeat known failures.

## Perfection without an endless cosmetic loop

“Best” requires a defined audience and promise. Seek a distinguished result: a clear original explanatory advantage, full substance, memorable mental models, disciplined delivery, and a usable artifact. Do not substitute an ever-growing deck for that result.

Continue while material gaps remain or a plausible change addresses a supported quality concern. Stop repeating a passed check unless something relevant changed. When all in-scope artifact obligations pass, deliver them with honest evidence limits rather than withholding useful work for an untestable universal perfection claim. Do not let enthusiasm for reach override accuracy, respect, or source integrity.

No automatic publishing, paid promotion, purchases, or scheduled future runs follow from the aspiration for a highly viewed video. The publication plan can be prepared within scope; external actions follow actual authorization.
