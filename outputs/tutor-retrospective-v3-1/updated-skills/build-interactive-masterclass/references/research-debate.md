# Broad discovery, deep inspection, original synthesis

Read for a flagship tutorial, masterclass, or substantial redesign. The user's named examples are seeds, not a closed source list. This user's quality preference requires a broad field survey before choosing the teaching route.

## 1. Build a landscape of at least 30 video candidates

For a substantial flagship explainer, discover at least 30 unique relevant YouTube/video candidates where they exist. Search multiple query families: plain-language intuition; visual explanation; derivation/proof; worked examples; implementation; applications/current practice; limitations/failure cases; alternatives; full university lectures; expert practitioner demonstrations. Expand terminology and appropriate languages when access supports meaningful evaluation.

Prioritize discoverable high-view and well-received explanations, while retaining technically strong, distinctive, or newer lower-view work. Include multiple creators and audience levels. Do not use 30 duplicates, clips of the same lesson, unrelated acronym matches, or 30 near-identical introductions to meet the number. A playlist is a discovery source; individual relevant lessons are candidates.

Log canonical URL/video ID, title, creator when verified, duration/date if available, topic/audience, source of metadata, access date, visible views/likes and how observed, and selection status. Missing metrics remain unknown. Relative views depend on age, channel, language, distribution, and topic size. Do not label a search result set the global top 30. “Rated” requires a named observed metric, not an invented quality score or inaccessible dislike ratio. Audience comments may reveal confusion but are not representative learning evidence.

Distinguish topic scarcity from blocked inspection. If 30 relevant videos can be identified but playback/transcripts are blocked, discovery can meet its floor while content review remains incomplete. Do not lower the discovery count to the number successfully played. If a niche topic genuinely yields fewer than 30 relevant candidates after broad queries, document the queries, unique count, access failures, and scarcity. Expand into original lectures, papers, books, demonstrations, or adjacent explanatory techniques; report those separately, without relabeling them as topic videos. Do not stall or fabricate a count. A short answer or small edit does not trigger a 30-video project.

## 2. Separate discovery, screening, and actual understanding

Use explicit access states:

- Discovered: metadata/search result only.
- Screened: inspected substantive transcript passages, creator text, or video segments sufficient to judge relevance and a proposed teaching move.
- Deeply analyzed: traced the relevant argument/sequence, examples, prerequisites, transitions, limits, and learner work.
- Visually reviewed: inspected actual representative visual states and their changes.
- Orally reviewed: listened to actual delivery for pacing, pauses, pronunciation, and synchronization.

These are overlapping evidence dimensions, not automatic upgrades. Captions do not prove visual or oral quality. A handful of thumbnails does not establish animation timing. Record timestamps/pages and access limits. Do not say “reviewed 30 videos” when only 30 URLs were found.

Screen the relevant candidate set and deeply analyze the strongest and most complementary routes. A shortlist of roughly 8–12 may be a useful starting allocation, but it is neither a ceiling nor a certificate. Expand when a key application, proof, limit, mitigation, alternative, or audience need remains unsupported. Inspect actual visual/oral segments before making claims about those qualities. Do not reduce the entire research stage to the three user-named presenters.

## 3. Extract teaching mechanisms, not scripts

For each inspected source identify:

- The promise, opening question, and purpose-to-method connection.
- The assumed starting model and diagnostic opportunities.
- Example lifecycle and representation choice.
- What insufficiency makes the next idea necessary.
- The hardest bridge and the precise move that supports it.
- Prerequisites introduced, claims justified/deferred, and analogy limits.
- What the learner predicts, constructs, explains, or diagnoses.
- Treatment of when/where, applications, limitations, mitigations, alternatives, and practice.
- Visual/oral techniques actually observed.
- A weakness, excluded audience, or missing obligation.
- The proposed design decision this source informs.

Use brief source-linked notes. Do not copy a teacher's script, voice, signature sequence, or protected visuals. Extract principles and develop original examples/diagrams. Track licenses and attribution for assets reused with permission.

## 4. Verify subject substance beyond video popularity

Use authoritative primary subject references for correctness and current claims. Read relevant methods, assumptions, proofs, evaluations, and limitations. Keep foundations and current applications separately dated. Search contrary evidence, failures, and strong alternatives, not only sources that support the preferred story.

A high-view video can be an excellent pacing reference while omitting the proof this learner needs. A recent technical paper can substantiate an application without providing an approachable teaching route. Give each source the job its evidence supports.

## 5. Synthesize a coherent route, not a best-moments collage

Prepare materially different serious routes. Normally begin with two or more; add another when an unresolved strategic choice warrants it. Each has a central learner question, coverage map, example/representation, bottlenecks, strongest objection, and a pilot that could reject it.

Compare techniques in a matrix: learner obligation → candidate teaching move → observed support → conflict with other moves → original adaptation → test. Choose a consistent notation, example lifecycle, voice, and visual grammar. Combining every admired technique can overload the lesson or produce contradictory assumptions.

Get separate initial proposals before sharing the coordinator's preference where possible. Cross-critique afterward. Preserve unresolved disagreement and minority objections. Author-informed and same-model reviews are not independent novice learning studies. Decide with observations, not vote count.

## 6. Benchmark the actual promise

Compare matched audience, prior knowledge, learning obligation, depth, and assessment. Use two complementary evaluations:

- A bounded matched-time comparison for a particular bridge, including navigation and video replay.
- A full-promise review for completeness, coherence, and independent capability, with actual learner time reported.

Equal-time testing is a comparison method, not a cap on the finished course. A 100-plus-slide masterclass can be justified; it cannot claim to beat a ten-minute introduction merely by covering more material. Nor should the central proof be removed to fit the comparator's length.

Separate correctness, explanation quality, preference, immediate performance, delayed recall, and transfer. Same-topic sequential exposure causes carryover; counterbalancing alone does not eliminate it. Use appropriate human study designs for comparative learning claims. AI judges must cite visible warrants and cannot establish what a novice learned.

## Research acceptance

The ledger, inspection notes, coverage map, dissent, and route decision must show why research changed the design. Candidate count is a breadth floor, not proof of quality. Uninspected or blocked content remains a gap. Research is ready for provisional authoring when important obligations have credible support and competing routes can be tested; it reopens when pilot evidence exposes a gap.

Read tutoring-evidence.md for learning-science constraints and narration-production.md for spoken/video evaluation. Never report future/source-study intentions as executed research.
