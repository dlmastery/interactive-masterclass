# Research, challenge, compare

## Inspect explanations, not reputations

For substantial courses, seek contrasting teaching routes (often three useful exemplars) and authoritative subject evidence. Stop when another source is unlikely to resolve the route's important uncertainty. Source count and view count are discovery signals, not learning scores.

Record URL/title, inspected pages or timestamps, access modality, purpose/opening, prerequisite assumptions, example/representation, hardest bridge, learner work, limits, and the decision informed. Metadata supports discovery; transcripts support wording and sequence; viewed segments support claims about those visuals and pacing. Do not claim to have watched unseen animation. Disclose inaccessible sources and use accessible alternatives.

Look for what makes an inference possible: an inadequacy exposed, invariant isolated, contrast constructed, representation mapped, or argument warranted. Do not copy a presenter's chapter order without checking audience and desired depth. Verify technical statements against primary sources. Synthesize original lessons.

## Competing routes and adjudication

For a full redesign, propose two materially different plausible routes. Each includes purpose, central sequence, bottleneck, likely failure, strongest objection, and pilot that could disconfirm it. Prefer separate initial proposals without the coordinator's favored answer. Cross-critique afterward. Label reused authors and same-context perspectives honestly.

Separate subject validity, teaching progression, and visual/interaction concerns; one reviewer may cover several if cost or staffing warrants. Preserve disagreement and decide which observation could resolve it. Do not average scores or incorporate every suggestion into a bloated hybrid. A cold whole-route walkthrough checks coherence after local repairs.

## Fair comparison with an expert tutorial

Define the bounded learning obligation before seeing results. Match audience preparation, promised depth, time including navigation, and outcome tasks. Allow video pause/replay. A short introductory video cannot be the sole comparator for a requested full proof; assemble a relevant reference path and disclose its scope.

Use unseen prediction, explanation, calculation/argument, boundary, and transfer tasks suited to the subject. Score assistance, correctness, reasoning, confidence, navigation burden, time, and preference separately. Hide author identity and blind scoring where feasible; disclose what remained recognizable.

AI judges are not novice learners. Require learner-visible warrants so prior topic knowledge cannot silently fill gaps. Fresh context reduces anchoring; shared models can still share errors. An A/B preference judgment is not a learning study. Same-topic sequential viewing has carryover; counterbalancing does not remove it. Actual comparative learning needs an appropriate human study design, such as separate randomized groups or matched tasks, with stated uncertainty. Small qualitative pilots identify defects; they do not establish universal superiority.

## Learning research: bounds on inference

Direct instruction, worked examples, guided experiments, and retrieval have different jobs. Novices may need a model before making useful predictions. Delayed recall evidence does not automatically establish conceptual transfer or efficiency.

When citing a study, record population, material, comparison, timing, measured outcome, and limits. Separate reported finding from proposed design implication. For example, Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Experiment 2, tested prose recall in undergraduates; its five-minute and one-week groups were separate, and study/recall periods differed in duration. Do not translate its percentages into universal retention, an AI advantage, or proof of a particular role workflow.

Do not impose a universal story, animation quota, quiz frequency, visual style, or interaction count. Let the learner obligation select the teaching method.
